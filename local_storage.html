How Browser Local Storage Works

Local Storage allows your browser to store data directly on your device for faster, safer, and offline access.

💾 What is Local Storage?

Local Storage is a feature in modern browsers that allows websites to store data directly on your device. Unlike cookies, it can store larger amounts of data and does not expire unless you manually clear it.

This makes it very useful for saving user preferences, app data, and offline content.

📡 Local Storage Workflow

This is how your data is stored using local storage. When you use a website or app, the data is saved directly in your browser instead of being sent to external servers. Only your browser can access this stored data. This makes it faster and more private. Even if someone intercepts network traffic, they cannot access this locally stored data. This ensures your data remains safe, private, and under your control.

⚙️ How Local Storage Works

Local Storage works using key-value pairs. Data is stored and retrieved using JavaScript.

localStorage.setItem("username", "Bibhas");
localStorage.getItem("username");

This data stays in your browser even after you close the tab or restart your system. It is only removed when you clear browser data or explicitly delete it.

Unlike cookies, local storage data is not sent to the server automatically with every request, making it more efficient and secure for storing non-sensitive data.

🛡 Why Local Storage is Important

✔ Stores data on your device
✔ Faster performance
✔ Works offline
✔ More storage than cookies
✔ Better for user experience
